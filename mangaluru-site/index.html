The city, in brief

A coastal capital built on trade

Mangaluru sits at the confluence of the Netravati and Gurupura rivers, on the Arabian Sea coast of southwestern Karnataka, backed by the forested slopes of the Western Ghats — one of the world's biodiversity hotspots. It is the administrative headquarters of Dakshina Kannada district and the largest city in the region.

Long before it was a modern city, this stretch of coast was a trading post for Arab, Persian and later European merchants drawn by spices and cashew. The name itself comes from Mangaladevi, the presiding deity of a temple built for a princess-turned-ascetic who settled here centuries ago — the city's oldest and most enduring story.

Today Mangaluru is one of India's more understated cosmopolitan cities: a working port, an education hub that draws students from across the country, and a launchpad for the beaches, temples and hill country that surround it. Tulu, Kannada, Konkani and Beary are all spoken on its streets, alongside English and Hindi.

Why come here

Four reasons to slow down in Mangaluru

It rarely gets the attention of Goa or Munnar — which is exactly the appeal.

Beaches without the crowds

Panambur, Tannirbhavi, Someshwar and Sasihithlu each have a distinct character, from jet-skiing and kite festivals to quiet ferry-crossing coves and surf breaks — see the Places to See page.

A layered religious history

Century-old Shiva temples, Dravidian-style shrines, colonial-era churches with hand-painted frescoes, and mosques sit within a few kilometres of each other.

A cuisine built on the coast

Neer dosa, ghee roast, kori rotti and a fish curry sharpened with kokum define Mangalorean food — read more on the Culture & Food page.

Easy to reach, easy to leave

An international airport, two railway stations, three national highways and a major sea port converge here — details on the Travel Info page.
