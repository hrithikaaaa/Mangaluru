Geography

Rivers, ghats and the sea

Mangaluru sits at roughly 12.87°N, 74.84°E, at the confluence of the Netravati and Gurupura rivers, with the Arabian Sea to its west and the forested Western Ghats rising to its east — a range recognised as one of the world's biodiversity hotspots.

It lies about 352 km west of Bengaluru, the state capital, roughly 20 km north of the Karnataka–Kerala border, and about 297 km south of Goa — putting it within reach of several other coastal and hill destinations.

The city has a tropical climate and sits in the direct path of the Arabian Sea branch of the south-west monsoon, which makes June through September the wettest — and greenest — stretch of the year.

Weather

When to visit

Three broad seasons shape the year here.

November to February — best time to visit

The monsoon has cleared, humidity drops, and days are warm and dry — the most comfortable stretch for sightseeing and the beaches.

March to May — hot and humid

Temperatures and humidity both climb ahead of the monsoon; mornings and evenings are more bearable than midday.

June to September — south-west monsoon

Heavy, sustained rain driven by the Arabian Sea monsoon branch. Dramatic scenery in the Ghats, but not ideal for beach time.

Getting here

All four modes of transport

Mangaluru is Karnataka's only city with air, rail, road and sea connections all in one place.

By air

Mangaluru International Airport (IXE) is at Bajpe, about 15–18 km from the city centre, with domestic connections to major Indian cities and international routes to the Gulf.

By rail

Two stations — Mangaluru Central and Mangaluru Junction — connect the city along the Konkan Railway and southern lines to Mumbai, Goa, Kerala and the rest of Karnataka.

By road

Three national highways meet here: one linking Panvel to Kanyakumari along the coast, one connecting Mangaluru to Bengaluru, and one running north-east to Solapur.

By sea

New Mangalore Port is the only major port in Karnataka, and one of India's busiest for coffee and cashew exports. The old harbour, Mangalore Bunder, is still used for fishing.

Good to know

Education, health & day-to-day

A few things that shape the character of the city beyond its sights.

Education hub. The city and its surrounding district are home to well-regarded engineering, medical and management institutions, drawing students from across India and abroad — a big part of why Mangaluru feels younger and more mixed than its size would suggest.

Clean and walkable. With broad roads and a compact centre, Mangaluru has consistently ranked among India's cleaner and less polluted cities, and most central sights are a short auto-rickshaw ride apart.
