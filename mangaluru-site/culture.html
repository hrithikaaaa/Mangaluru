People & language

A city of several tongues

Mangaluru sits inside the region known as Tulu Nadu, and its everyday speech reflects it. Most residents move between two or three languages without thinking about it.

On the plate

Mangalorean cuisine

Coastal, coconut-heavy and built around rice, the food here is distinct from cuisine elsewhere in Karnataka — shaped by Tuluva, Konkani, Bunt, Mangalorean Catholic and Beary kitchens.

Neer Dosa

A thin, delicate rice crepe made from a watery batter — usually eaten plain with coconut milk or a light curry.

Mangalore Buns

Sweet, banana-flavoured deep-fried buns, usually served alongside coconut chutney or sambar as a snack.

Ghee Roast

Chicken, prawn or mushroom cooked in a fiery, tangy red masala finished with generous ghee — a modern Mangalorean signature.

Fish Curry

Built on a coconut and red chilli base, sharpened with kokum instead of tamarind in many home kitchens along this coast.

Kori Rotti

A Tuluva classic: crisp rice wafers soaked in a spicy chicken curry, eaten with your hands.

Pathrode

Colocasia leaves layered with a spiced rice-and-lentil paste, rolled and steamed — a Konkani and Tuluva staple.

Festivals & performance

What's worth timing your trip around

Between temple festivals, harvest celebrations and folk theatre, there is rarely a quiet month here.

Kambala

Traditional buffalo races held across paddy fields in the region through the cooler months — a deeply local sporting tradition rather than a tourist show.

Yakshagana

A centuries-old theatre form combining dance, music, elaborate costume and improvised dialogue, drawn from Hindu epics — performed through the night at temple festivals.

Bhuta Kola

A ritual form of spirit worship unique to Tulu Nadu, in which performers embody local deities through costume, fire and dance.

Kite Festival, Panambur Beach

An international kite-flying festival held on the beach, drawing flyers and spectators from across the country.

Monti Fest

A harvest festival celebrated by the city's Mangalorean Catholic community, marking the birth of Mother Mary alongside the new paddy harvest.
