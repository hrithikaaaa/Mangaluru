Where the name comes from

Named for a goddess

Local legend holds that a princess of Malabar, Parimaladevi, renounced her kingdom to become a disciple of Matsyendranath, founder of the Nath tradition. He renamed her Mangaladevi, and she settled near present-day Bolar — the temple built in her honour gave the city its name. One of the earliest written references to the city, as "Managalapuram," comes from a Pandya inscription dated to 715 CE.

The region around the city is known locally as Tulu Nadu, after the predominance of the Tulu language, and the city carries several names across the languages spoken here: Kudla in Tulu, Mangaluru in Kannada, Kodial in Konkani, and Mangalapuram in Malayalam.

A timeline

Centuries of trade and rule

Few Indian port cities changed hands as often as Mangaluru — each ruling power left something behind, from architecture to place names.

Early dynasties

The Kadambas and the Alupas, a local dynasty that governed the Tulu coast for centuries, were among the earliest recorded rulers of the region, which prospered as a trading post on the Arabian Sea spice route.

Vijayanagar & Keladi era

Control passed to the Vijayanagar Empire and later the Keladi Nayaks, under whom the port continued to draw Arab and European traders dealing in pepper, cashew and coconut.

Portuguese arrival

European trading interest brought a Portuguese presence to the coast, adding the first layer of colonial-era architecture and churches to the city's skyline.

Hyder Ali & Tipu Sultan

The city became a point of contention between the Kingdom of Mysore, under Hyder Ali and later Tipu Sultan, and the British East India Company, changing hands repeatedly during the Anglo-Mysore Wars.

1799 — British annexation

Following Tipu Sultan's defeat, the British annexed the city and folded it into the Madras Presidency, where it remained for nearly 150 years.

1947 — Independence

Mangaluru became part of independent India within Madras State, continuing its role as the region's principal port and market town.

1956 — States Reorganisation

Under the States Reorganisation Act, the city and its district were transferred from Madras State into the newly enlarged Mysore State, which was renamed Karnataka in 1973.

Present day

Mangaluru is now the administrative headquarters of Dakshina Kannada district, home to the New Mangalore Port — the only major port in Karnataka, handling a large share of India's coffee and cashew exports — and a fast-growing base for education, healthcare and IT.
